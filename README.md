# What 438 FDA Complete Response Letters Actually Say

<p align="center"><b>438</b> letters classified &nbsp;·&nbsp; <b>~half</b> cite facility readiness &nbsp;·&nbsp; <b>211</b> CGMP / 483 citations &nbsp;·&nbsp; <b>2024</b> the peak year</p>

**One dataset behind the FDA’s refusals: 438 Complete Response Letters, every one classified against its own wording. Here is what the numbers say.**

<img src="assets/hero.jpg" alt="An operator inspecting a vial above a pharmaceutical filling line in a cleanroom" width="100%">

> The most common quality reason the FDA refuses a drug application is no longer the science; it is the plant, and the agency says so in near-identical sentences, letter after letter. This report classifies every Complete Response Letter the FDA has published, a total of 438 refusals spanning from 2002 to 2026, against the letters' own wording: eleven rejection categories, sixteen GMP sub-themes, trended by year. Inside are the four sentences the agency reuses across dozens of refusals, the twelve-year view showing facility readiness climbing while the classic CMC themes fade, and the two ways this record is routinely misread, including why the labelling numbers mean the opposite of what they seem to. It closes with six specific checks a quality team can run against its next filing, drawn from the letters rather than from general principle.
