> The most common reason the FDA refuses a drug application is no longer the science behind the drug; it is the quality practices in a facility. Letter after letter, the agency is saying so in near-identical sentences. This report classifies every Complete Response Letter the FDA has published, a total of 438 refusals spanning from 2002 to 2026. Using state-of-the-art semantic models, we arrived at eleven rejection categories and sixteen GMP sub-themes, trended by year.
>
> Inside you will find the four most common sentences the agency cites again and again for these refusals.
>
> The twelve-year view is broken down into themes, showing facility readiness climbing while the classic CMC themes fade gradually.
>
> You will be surprised to learn why the labelling numbers mean the opposite of what they seem to, and the two ways these CRLs are routinely misread.
>
> Finally, it closes with six specific checks a quality team can run before its next filing, drawn from the specifics found in these letters.
